## The architecture: two layers

**Scenes** are 3D content: pages of their own, where the depth is real
geometry. They are the training ground and the MVP (the owner, 15:24).
**Looks** are 3D as a display mode: any page draws its ordinary 2D frame,
nothing about it changes, and the chosen look shows that frame in 3D (the
owner, 15:22). Both run on one model, `src/fx3d/`, and both work in mono and
with the glasses.

```
page's 2D frame --capture--> look  --+
                                     +--> mono, or left+right --> compose --> encode (CIE) --> blit
scene (real 3D) ---------------------+
```

1. *Scene or look*: 3D coordinates and a time. Both eyes see the same moment.
2. *Projection*: mono, or off-axis stereo - parallel cameras at x = ±b/2 and a
   zero-parallax plane at Z0 (the brief §4). No toe-in.
3. *Raster*: into **linear-light** buffers - points at sub-pixel positions, Wu
   lines, integer-edge triangles with a depth test, per-pixel rays for the
   fly-throughs.
4. *Compose*: mono colour, or anaglyph (red-blue first, red-cyan, red-green),
   with per-eye gains and an eye swap. Additive: one eye never erases the other.
5. *Encode once*: linear light → the CIE lightness code the library expects,
   through the inverse of the library's own table. No double gamma.
6. *Blit*: 128×64 from PSRAM with `drawPixelRGB888`, like the Lua effects.

**Depth budget.** A point at depth Z has disparity d = f·b·(1/Z − 1/Z0)
pixels. A scene declares its depth range and b is chosen so |d| stays within
`depthPx`, which starts at 2 (the brief §4: 1-2 px, an experiment setting,
not a norm). Vertices are never clamped one by one. The looks that move pixels
spend `depthPx` directly.

### The looks (8 and flat)

| Look | Family | How |
|---|---|---|
| `flat` | - | the page as it is |
| `pop` | glasses | brighter is nearer; black stays on the panel's plane |
| `layers` | glasses | warm colours in front, white in the middle, cold behind (a design choice, not a measured rule) |
| `float` | glasses | the whole picture one plane in front - the brief's "only a flat layer", kept as the baseline |
| `dome` | glasses | the middle bulges towards the viewer |
| `wiggle` | no glasses | the `pop` depth seen from an eye swaying sideways about twice a second |
| `card` | no glasses | the picture on a card swaying in space |
| `relief` | no glasses | lit pixels raised as blocks, their sides in shade, oblique projection |
| `drum` | no glasses | wrapped round a drum turning to and fro |

The glasses looks move each pixel sideways by its own disparity, per eye,
row by row: same-depth pixels tile and add, depth bands are laid far to near,
uncovered pixels stay black. `card`, `relief` and `drum` are real geometry,
so they work with the glasses too. Tried on six real pages on the host
(`tools/fx3d/looks.py`): the snake, tetris and world clocks, Minecraft, the
snooker clock and the weather screen. Big digits on black are where the
glasses looks read best; on a full lit picture `pop` becomes `float`.

### How a look reaches any page on the panel (next step)

The DMA frame buffer is private in ESP32-HUB75-MatrixPanel-DMA 3.0.14, so the
frame is captured where it is drawn, not read back. Under the flag the global
`display` becomes a subclass (`src/fx3d/`) that overrides the library's five
virtual drawing calls (`drawPixel`, `fillScreen`, `fillRect`,
`drawFastVLine`, `drawFastHLine`) and hides `drawPixelRGB888`,
`fillScreenRGB888`, `clearScreen`, `clearDisplay` and `display()`. Every
page reaches the panel through those: 111 `print`, 199 `fillRect`, 190
`drawPixel`… across `src/`, and no code holds a base-class pointer to
`display`. While a look is on, the calls fill a PSRAM copy of the frame; at
`display()` the look turns it into the 3D frame, which then goes to the
panel. With the look off, the calls pass straight through. With the flag off,
nothing changes.

### The glasses profile in NVS (what the panel stores)

What the owner sets with the calibration survives a reboot (`feat/fx3d`
`5bdc8e0`). NVS namespace **`fx3d`**, one typed key per value, so any NVS
dump shows them:

| key | type | meaning | range |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mode` | u8 | 0 mono, 1 red-blue, 2 red-cyan, 3 red-green | 0..3 |
| `swap` | u8 | 1: the red channel shows the right eye's picture | 0..1 |
| `depth` | u16 | the largest disparity, in 1/100 of a pixel | 0..1600 |
| `gl` | u8 | the left eye's gain, per cent | 0..100 |
| `gr` | u8 | the right eye's gain, per cent | 0..100 |
| `ver` | u8 | this layout, written last | 1 |

- **Nothing stored** is the defaults: red-blue, the eyes as they are, 2 px,
  both eyes at 100 %. `/api/fx3d?profile=reset` (the page's «Вернуть
  исходные») erases the namespace, so a later firmware's defaults apply too.
- **Reading never refuses** (the integration's request: the panel lives in a
  wall). A key that is missing, of another type or out of its range gives
  that value's default; a `ver` other than 1, or none, gives every default.
- **Writing** waits 2.5 s after the last change (the clock styles'
  `SETTLE_MS`) and never runs while the bench borrows the profile. Only the
  keys that changed are written, `ver` last. A key of ours found with
  another type makes the next write erase the namespace and write every key:
  in ESP-IDF 4.4.7 (arduino-esp32 2.0.17) a put of another type does not
  replace the old entry, and reads on that page keep finding the old one
  (`nvs_page.cpp`, `nvs_storage.cpp`, `nvs_types.cpp` at v4.4.7). A refused
  write is tried again three times, one settle apart (our choice), then waits
  for the next change.
- **What it says:** `/api/fx3d` answers `"profile":"kept"`, `"pending"` or
  `"failed"`; the log prints `[fx3d] glasses from NVS, 5 of 5 values: ...` at
  boot and `[fx3d] glasses saved, N keys in M ms` per write.
- **Not stored:** the look and the scene - a reboot brings the panel back
  flat, on its page. The portal's factory reset clears `pcmonitor` only; the
  glasses profile survives it, as the other modules' namespaces do.
